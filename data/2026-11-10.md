Abstract: Subliminal learning is a phenomenon in large language models (LLMs) where a teacher passes a hidden preference to a student through training data unrelated to that preference. We study whether subliminal learning also occurs in vision-language models (VLMs). We finetune a teacher VLM to prefer a specific animal or facial attribute and use a control teacher from the same base model. Both teachers generate numbers for an unrelated visual task involving geometric shapes. Two students initialized from the same base VLM are finetuned on the same images, using numbers generated by either the biased or control teacher. Across VLMs ranging from 2B to 7B parameters, we find that both animal and facial preferences can transfer through this unrelated task. Students trained on biased-teacher outputs select the target more often than control students, with a difference of up to $\sim$10 percentage points in the animal preference task. Transfer can become stronger with more training data and varies with the target preference, model, and how the numbers are generated. Beyond preferences, we show that the biased student's explanations use more of the same words and phrases as the biased teacher's explanations than those of the control student, even when all three models select the same image. These results matter for training VLMs and show that training on unrelated data may change both a VLM's image preferences and how it explains its choices, and that transfer can become stronger with more training samples.

Abstract: Geospatial foundation models (GFMs) are reshaping remote sensing by shifting the field from task-specific modeling toward general-purpose geospatial intelligence. Abstract: We propose, HaNDF, object-conditional neural distance fields as data-driven priors for modeling the plausible hand-object configurations, in the product manifold of hand articulations. Abstract: Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) are the dominant architecture for video generation, but their quadratic attention complexity results in substantial computational cost and latency. Permuted block-sparse attention (PBSA) offers a promising approach to reducing this cost by selectively computing blocks of a semantically permuted attention matrix. However, existing approaches typically select only the top few key blocks for each query block, following a top-$p$ paradigm. We find that top-$p$ selection can be highly sample inefficient in high-entropy attention regimes, which are common in video generation. In these regimes, we show that sampling can provide substantially better approximations under the same computational budget. More generally, we cast block selection as importance sampling, where the top-scoring keys form an exact set that is computed in full and the remaining keys are represented by a sample drawn in proportion to their estimated attention and reweighted by inverse sampling probability. Building on this formulation, we present InstaGen, which can be parameterized by any PBSA method: it replaces the method's block selection with an exact set plus a weighted sample. Each query cluster draws one sample shared by all its queries, so the sampled keys form blocks amenable to efficient GPU execution. Existing compression methods often couple content selection with budget determination. We observe that neighboring slices share visual features and that attention concentration varies across visual encoders. We propose VoluCompose, a training-free, model-agnostic framework that separates what visual content to retain from how many tokens to allocate.
