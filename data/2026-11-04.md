Abstract: Reconstructing physical dynamics from fixed sparse sensors and missing frames is a fundamental challenge in scientific research. Sparsity leaves large regions of the field unconstrained, making the inverse problem ill-posed: a model may fit the observed sensors while producing inaccurate off-sensor structures or unreliable predictions between support frames. To this end, we propose PhysFTM---a physics-informed functional Tucker model that parameterizes the Tucker mode factors in reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHSs) and incorporates PDE constraints through finite-difference residuals evaluated at off-sensor collocation points. These residuals provide additional physical information in regions without direct observational supervision. PhysFTM first reconstructs continuous spatial fields at support frames via an alternating minimization scheme, optimizing the Tucker core tensor and the RKHS-based factors separately. To enable missing-frame reconstruction, PhysFTM constructs a continuous trajectory in Tucker-core space, which is decoded by the learned spatial RKHS-FTM representation to recover the full spatiotemporal field. Experiments on Allen--Cahn, Navier--Stokes, three-dimensional Heat equations, and a three-dimensional gridded Copernicus ocean reanalysis demonstrate accurate 2D/3D reconstruction under two missing-frame protocols. Code is available at https://github.com/liusirzzz/PhysFTM.

Abstract: Open-set object detection (OSOD) suffers from a fundamental limitation under dense proposal mechanisms: highly overlapping proposals associated with the same object are often assigned contradictory known/unknown predictions. Existing methods perform open-set reasoning independently for each proposal, while ignoring the shared semantics among correlated proposals, resulting in fragmented detections and unstable open-set decisions. In this paper, we reformulate OSOD as an object-centric semantic inference problem, where correlated proposals jointly support a coherent region-level semantic decision. Based on this perspective, we propose OCSI-Net, a unified framework that combines object-centric semantic consistency with geometry-aware uncertainty stabilization. Specifically, OCSI-Net aggregates correlated proposals into structured region clusters and infers region-level semantics from aggregated semantic evidence, thereby enforcing coherent open-set predictions within the same object region. Furthermore, a geometry-aware uncertainty mechanism is introduced to stabilize optimization near open-set transition boundaries. Extensive experiments on multiple benchmarks demonstrate that OCSI-Net consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance, improving the harmonic mean of known and unknown detection by an average of 6.83\% over the second-best method.

Abstract: Effective world modeling requires compressing salient history information that facilitates future prediction.
