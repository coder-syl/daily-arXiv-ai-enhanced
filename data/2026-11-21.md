Abstract: Understanding an egocentric environment requires reasoning about how surrounding entities affect the observer's actions. Existing evaluations of multimodal foundation models (MFMs) examine observer localization, motion trajectories, and action feasibility, but leave the discovery and interpretation of evolving observer-entity interactions insufficiently assessed. We introduce EgoPass-Bench, a benchmark for observer-relative interaction understanding in real-world egocentric video. Abstract: Humans can understand the 3D structure of scenes even from highly unfamiliar or extreme viewpoints. Cognitive science suggests this generalization stems from a top-down generative inference strategy. In contrast, state-of-the-art 3D reconstruction neural networks typically rely on amortized, feedforward inference. While fast, this pattern-matching approach degrades when faced with out-of-distribution (OOD) scene conditions unseen during training. Inspired by the generative nature of human vision, we propose Infer3D, an inverse generative modeling framework that recovers the latent variables of a generative model to best match the monocular input. We propose VisAlloc, a dual-level framework combining sample-level visual filtering with token-level counterfactual reweighting. Abstract: Localizing object boundaries is important for embodied visual systems, since small boundary errors can change the inferred geometry of physical surfaces. We study whether deep stereo models produce boundaries consistent with simple shape priors when local image and disparity cues are weak. We create a synthetic stereo dataset consisting of objects drawn from a highly constrained family of shapes, with randomized depth, slant, color and texture. Despite the simplicity of this shape space, we find that several state-of-the-art stereo models retain systematic boundary errors, even when finetuned or trained from scratch on the dataset. We then introduce a simple generative stereo model that jointly predicts disparity and a distance field representing object boundaries. The model improves boundary localization and produces contours that adhere more closely to the underlying shape space. These results expose limitations in the boundary predictions of current stereo architectures when local evidence is weak, and motivate further study of whether generative models can better combine regional grouping evidence with global geometric structure.

Abstract: Human-object motion synthesis requires coordinated human participation as objects move toward specified goals. Abstract: A calibrated decision model can predict how often each answer is chosen. The same prediction can reflect disagreement among people who are confident in their choices or uncertainty among people who share a belief. Disagreement calls for identifying whose perspective applies, while uncertainty calls for gathering more evidence. Standard post-training scores a single prediction against one observed choice at a time, learning the pooled distribution without distinguishing these two sources of uncertainty. We introduce Belief-Grouped Policy Optimization (BGPO). The policy proposes a list of beliefs, estimates their prevalence, and predicts a choice distribution for each. During training, BGPO uses Bayes' rule to estimate how likely each belief was to generate an observed choice. These estimates weight the choice's contribution to each belief's log-score reward. To discover rare beliefs, the policy also proposes lists after seeing a few observed choices and retains them only if they predict held-out choices well. At inference, the policy uses only the question and combines the belief-specific distributions by prevalence to produce a calibrated aggregate prediction. On benchmarks covering opinions, conversation safety, emotions elicited by artwork, and photo aesthetics, BGPO achieves the lowest conditional, worst-group, and overall Jensen-Shannon divergence across model initializations. It improves conditional divergence on both text and image tasks. On a synthetic image task with known beliefs, BGPO recovers the underlying groups more accurately than baselines trained with belief labels.

Abstract: Simulated manipulation benchmarks measure policy performance using hand-written success checkers that label each episode as a success or failure. Passing an expert demonstration shows that a checker accepts one way of completing the task. Learned policies, however, may stop after partial progress or complete the task in a different way. A checker may then accept an incomplete execution (phantom success) or reject an execution that satisfies the instruction (silent failure). In this paper, we introduce SimAudit, an LLM-based pipeline that audits success checkers against task instructions. SimAudit analyzes the instruction and checker code to identify scenarios where the two may disagree, recreates those scenarios in simulation, and runs the original checker. Multi-view renderings are then used to verify whether the observed behavior satisfies the instruction. Across RoboTwin 2.0, RoboCasa365, and RoboDojo, 183 simulated test cases produced the predicted checker verdict. Most defects in RoboCasa365 accepted incomplete executions, most in RoboDojo rejected valid executions, and both error types were common in RoboTwin 2.0. These findings suggest that validating success checkers requires testing both incomplete executions and valid alternatives to expert demonstrations, and motivate more rigorous evaluation protocols for future benchmarks.

Abstract: Whole-body human recognition has advanced rapidly with large vision models. However, existing methods remain fragile under real-world variations, as they tie identity to transient cues such as clothing, texture, pose, or viewpoint.
