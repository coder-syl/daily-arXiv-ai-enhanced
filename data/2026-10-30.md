Abstract: Measurements of tree diameter at breast height (DBH) are fundamental to forest inventories, but manual surveys across entire stands or regions are impractical. UAV-borne laser scanning (ULS) enables scalable automated forest surveys, yet canopy occlusion makes accurate DBH estimation from ULS point clouds non-trivial. Limited stem-level point density of ULS data constrains approaches relying on detailed stem reconstruction, such as Quantitative Structure Models (QSMs) and circle fitting. Empirical machine learning methods based on predefined point cloud metrics are an alternative. New deep learning approaches offer an even more principled solution, allowing models to learn directly from point clouds. Sparse tensor networks have shown particular promise, transforming forestry tasks such as tree species classification. Segmentation quality emerges as the dominant error source in the automated pipeline. TreeNext proves highly data-efficient, performing well even in small data scenarios. Our findings establish sparse tensor networks as a practical foundation for automated individual tree DBH estimation from ULS.

Abstract: Deepfake detectors routinely achieve high aggregate accuracy while exhibiting substantial demographic disparities in their errors: false-positive rates differ by double digits across gender and skin tone. Abstract: A widely held assumption in knowledge distillation is that teacher and student representations must occupy a compatible or explicitly aligned feature space. We propose RPKD (Random Prototype Knowledge Distillation), which discards this assumption by comparing both networks against a shared bank of randomly initialized, frozen prototype vectors. Alignment is therefore carried out in an external reference space rather than in the teacher's native feature coordinates: the teacher side is projected by fixed random maps that carry no trainable parameters, and only the student adapts. Two branches handle the transfer: a logit branch aligning global prototype similarity distributions and a feature branch matching spatially-resolved prototype activation maps. Both operate within the same fixed prototype space, which can be interpreted as a random feature embedding that approximately preserves inter-sample similarity structure, so the prototype bank and the distillation objective stay unchanged across teacher-student pairs while only the input projections are instantiated per architecture. In low-category settings, decoupling the prototype vocabulary from the task label space yields richer supervision than the label space alone, an advantage absent from logit-based methods, whose supervisory signal collapses as class count shrinks. This advantage is especially relevant in real-world scenarios where the label space is typically constrained. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of RPKD, achieving a maximum gain of +7.98% over OFA on CIFAR-100, +12.44% on ImageNet-100, and +1.28% on ImageNet-1K.
